## Description: 
This analysis was conducted to evaluate the hospital’s operational efficiency and identify key areas for improvement.

## Process:
Cleaned and standardized raw hospital data by formatting date, time, and numerical fields. Removed duplicates and missing values, corrected typos and errors, and resolved columns containing multiple inconsistent data points.

Created pivot tables to analyze trends and relationships across key operational areas, including appointments, billing, patients, insurance providers, and treatments.

Identified patterns and performance gaps in appointment attendance, payment completion rates, and the available data on provider and treatment effectiveness.

## Data:
This dataset includes five CSV files:

* patients.csv – Patient demographics, contact details, registration info, and insurance data
* doctors.csv – Doctor profiles with specializations, experience, and contact information
* appointments.csv – Appointment dates, times, visit reasons, and statuses
* treatments.csv – Treatment types, descriptions, dates, and associated costs
* billing.csv – Billing amounts, payment methods, and status linked to treatments

## Assumptions:
All data was downloaded from Kaggle and synthetically generated for educational and project use. 

No real patient information was used for this analysis.

## Findings:
The findings highlight three critical areas for improvement:

* Appointment Management – Over half of all scheduled appointments result in cancellations or no-shows, indicating a need to streamline scheduling systems and communication processes.
* Billing and Payment Processing – With 68% of payments pending or failed, the hospital should review its payment infrastructure to identify and address inefficiencies.
* Provider Performance Metrics – Current data lacks insight into quality of care. Incorporating patient feedback and outcome-based measures can enhance evaluations of doctors, treatments, and insurance providers.
